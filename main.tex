\title{How Far Can Heuristic Learning Go? \\
Characterizing Performance Ceilings, Complexity, and Sample Efficiency in Large-Scale Real-World Competitive Games}

\author{THU-CST-SAST}

\abstract{Symbolic systems --- heuristic rules and search --- are widely held to
lack the extensibility of connectionist ones: a symbolic program exhibits only
behaviour someone wrote down, while a learned policy acquires behaviour its
designer never specified. Coding agents unsettle that premise. A symbolic program
is the artifact an agent can maintain --- readable, diffable, revisable in place
--- while a learned policy hides in its weights and admits no such loop. How far,
then, does the symbolic paradigm now go? Auditing the complete archive of a
collegiate game AI contest running since \val{founded} (\val{editions} editions,
\val{nsub} submissions, \val{nmatch} evaluated matches) shows the received
ordering describing asymptotic ceilings correctly while failing to predict who
wins: \val{pheur} of prize-winning programs contain no learned component, and RL,
present in \val{prl} of submissions, reached the finals in \val{nrlfinal} of
\val{editions} editions. Contests are decided under a budget,
where the binding constraint is the width of the feedback channel rather than the
expressiveness of the policy class --- rules and replays carry far more
information per sample than a scalar reward. Human heuristics were thus bounded
by the cost of human iteration, not by the paradigm, a confound that has left its
ceiling unmeasured. We remove it with \emph{heuristic learning} (HL): a coding
agent maintains a heuristic program by reading the rules, its own source, and
replays of its losses, revising, and rescoring against a frozen opponent pool.
Because the archive supplies a gradient of human skill rather than a single
baseline, HL can be placed within the human field: it reaches the \val{pct}th
percentile across \val{ngames} games and surpasses the strongest recorded human on
\val{nbeat}. It is \val{effratio} more sample-efficient than model-based RL and
policy gradient at matched Elo, though RL overtakes beyond \val{crossover}
environment steps and HL stays costlier \val{loseaxis}. Improvement frequency
decays as \val{decay} and plateaus near \val{plateau} revisions, a ceiling we
trace to \val{ceilingcause}.}
