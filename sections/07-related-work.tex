\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Programs as policies, and who writes them.}
Programmatic reinforcement learning represents a policy as an executable program rather
than an opaque network, and bounds the size of an optimal programmatic policy --- the
formal relative of the description length this paper measures
\citep{programmaticrl2024foundations}. Language models have since been used to synthesize
such policies \citep{llmguidedsearch2024} and to search structured domains such as
scheduling \citep{diprl2026,prorl2026}, while automatic heuristic design pairs model
proposals with evolutionary selection, uncertainty balancing, diversity preservation, or
learned teachers \citep{qube2024,hsevo2024,teacherawareheuristic2026,heuristicsearch2026,
latentheuristicsearch2026,automodsat2025}. A parallel line writes code around a learner
rather than as the learner: reward functions \citep{eureka2023,rewarddesignagent2026}, and
the post-training machinery of reinforcement learning itself
\citep{agent2rlbench2026} --- which is what makes ``then let it write the policy'' the
next question rather than a contrarian one. On the search side, FunSearch established the
pattern of model-generated variants filtered by an external evaluator
\citep{funsearch2024}, extended by systems that make proposal, mutation, repair, and
verification autonomous \citep{alphaevolve2025,avo2026}, by recursive self-improvement of
coding agents \citep{selfimprovingcodingagent2025,darwingodelmachine2025,mendelgoedel2026},
and by iterative refinement from self-generated feedback alone \citep{selfrefine2023}. How
much of the reported gain belongs to the harness rather than the method is itself now
measured \citep{harnessengineering2026}, which is why ours is held fixed. The closest
game-specific systems use model-controlled program search
\citep{codetoplay2024,kuang2025generative}. These optimize a fixed objective, or a
population, or the scaffolding; we maintain one artifact across many revisions under
adversarial play, recording both its strength and its description length.

\paragraph{Adversarial evaluation and human fields.}
Self-play and model-based planning established games as a demanding testbed for learned
agents \citep{alphazero2018,muzero2020,alphastar2019,openaifive2019}, and code-agent
evaluation has moved toward tournaments and iterative competition
\citep{vibecoding2025tournament,catarena2025}. Co-evolutionary work grows a strategy pool
against which heuristics are discovered \citep{asro2026coevolution}, and
adversarial-instance generation improves the evaluator instead of the policy
\citep{adversarialheuristics2026}; our test pool is frozen instead, buying attributable
rating changes at the cost of co-evolutionary pressure. What the contest archive adds is a
human field rather than a reference opponent, which is what licenses placing a method as a
percentile of real contestants --- the protocol used for Kaggle competitions and olympiad
fields \citep{mlebench2024,olympiad2025percentile}. Placement also requires the right
learned baseline: comparing against policy gradient alone would confound symbolic against
neural with on-policy against off-policy, since the loop learns from any opponent's
replays and from human source, so the primary baseline is off-policy and model-based
\citep{offpolicyvaluellm2026,samplereuse2022}.

\paragraph{Continual improvement and its ceiling.}
Memory-based agents improve without changing the program or the weights, by rewriting
failed trajectories, retaining verbal lessons, or distilling deployment feedback into rules
\citep{echo2025hindsight,reflexion2023,learningonthejob2026}, and recent benchmarks argue
that continual learning must measure transfer and interference rather than long-context
retrieval \citep{agentcl2026,clbench2026}. In heuristic learning experience is not
retrieved but written into the artifact, which is what creates the failure mode this paper
measures. The mechanism is accumulation: revisions leave the program larger and more
interconnected \citep{mccabe1976complexity}, and each further revision must place an edit
without disturbing what already works, until past some size placement fails more often
than it succeeds --- not because the paradigm ran out of things to express, but because the
artifact has outgrown what its maintainer can hold, attribute, and change at once. Each
term of that account is now measured outside our setting. Across 196 checkpoints of
iterative tasks, erosion rises in 77\% of trajectories and verbosity in 75.5\%, at 2.2$\times$
the cost per checkpoint and without matching gains in correctness; human repositories are
not exempt but degrade an order of magnitude more slowly, so capacity is a rate rather
than an agent-specific defect \citep{slopcodebench2026}. Quality-aware prompting moves the
intercept and leaves the growth rate intact, at a cost in correctness, which is evidence
for a capacity rather than a prompting artifact. Two mechanisms underneath are not about
code quality: agentic repair succeeds while the context each step accumulates stays small
\citep{longcontextbugfix2026}, and per-step accuracy falls as steps accumulate even when
the plan is given, though reasoning removes the self-conditioning behind it
\citep{longhorizonexecution2026} --- so a plateau reached by a reasoning agent cannot be
attributed to that effect. A program under revision is the case that cannot be decomposed
away, since the artifact \emph{is} the accumulated context. One boundary keeps these
results from predicting that our loop cannot work at all: the degradation they measure is
driven by a specification that keeps growing, so nothing may be discarded, whereas a
game's rules are fixed and a better heuristic may \emph{replace} a worse one. Accumulation
is a tendency here rather than an obligation, which is why the ceiling is worth measuring
instead of assuming. Frequency and magnitude of improvement do both decay on real
engineering tasks \citep{frontiereng2026}, but those tasks are single-agent and carry
neither a human field nor a learned baseline, leaving the position of the symbolic paradigm
against a learned one open; a survey of this literature organizes it by the strength of the
verification signal the loop closes on \citep{selfimprovement2026survey}, at whose strong
end a game engine with a frozen pool sits. We carry the same longitudinal question into
adversarial games and ask whether the plateau is explained by the maintainable description
length of the policy rather than by the complexity of the game's rules.
