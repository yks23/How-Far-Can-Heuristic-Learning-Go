\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Reinforcement learning on adversarial games.}
Symbolic construction won adversarial games first, by search over a hand-authored
evaluation \citep{deepblue2002}. Learned value estimation displaced it by removing one
hand-authored component after another --- the evaluation function, the human game record,
the game-specific method, finally the environment model
\citep{dqn2015,alphago2016,alphagozero2017,alphazero2018,muzero2020,efficientzero2021} ---
and then extended into the settings our suite occupies: imperfect information by
equilibrium computation or model-free multiagent learning, multiplayer and non-zero-sum
payoffs, hidden hands over long horizons, real-time control against champion humans, and
action spaces that defeat enumeration
\citep{cfr2007,deepstack2017,libratus2018,pluribus2019,deepnash2022,suphx2020,douzero2021,
gtsophy2022,alphastar2019,openaifive2019}. Where a language model has entered these games
it negotiated beside a planner rather than authoring the policy \citep{cicero2022}. Two
results bear on how we evaluate: opponent pools are a design variable, and superhuman
average strength can still hide holes an adversary finds
\citep{psro2017,adversarialgo2023,asro2026coevolution,adversarialheuristics2026} --- hence
a frozen test pool drawn from a whole human field rather than one strong reference, with
cost reported beside strength
\citep{katago2019,mlebench2024,olympiad2025percentile}. This paradigm's standing is not in
question. What it leaves open is the small-budget regime, and against which learner:
policy gradient alone would confound symbolic against neural with on-policy against
off-policy, so our primary baseline is off-policy and model-based
\citep{offpolicyvaluellm2026,samplereuse2022}.

\paragraph{Policies written as programs.}
A policy can be an executable program rather than an opaque network, and before language
models the program came from a learner --- a sketch filled by policy search, a decision
tree distilled for verifiability, a searchable latent space of programs, symbolic
controllers recovered directly
\citep{pirl2018,viper2018,inductivegen2020,leaps2021,symbolicpolicies2021}. The
representation's size--expressiveness trade-off has since been made formal, bounding an
optimal programmatic policy's size, the relative of the description length measured here
\citep{programmaticrl2024foundations}; learning has also been aimed at code as the artifact,
discovering algorithms that were then adopted \citep{alphatensor2022,alphadev2023}. Language
models changed who writes the program: they synthesize policies, search structured domains,
and supply proposals that evolutionary selection, uncertainty balancing, diversity
preservation, or learned teachers filter
\citep{llmguidedsearch2024,qube2024,hsevo2024,diprl2026,prorl2026,teacherawareheuristic2026,
heuristicsearch2026,latentheuristicsearch2026,automodsat2025}. A parallel branch writes code
around a learner instead, from reward functions to post-training machinery
\citep{eureka2023,rewarddesignagent2026,agent2rlbench2026,ai4aibench2026}. AI4AI-Bench
moves this idea up to algorithmic design, asking an agent to rewrite the training
procedure itself; that is complementary to our question, which asks whether an agent
can maintain the executable policy being trained. This makes ``then let it write the
policy'' the next question rather than a contrarian one, taken up by
model-controlled program search \citep{codetoplay2024,kuang2025generative}. In all of these
the program is a search output: extracted, sampled, or selected from a population. In ours
it is a maintained artifact revised in place, which is what makes its description length a
trajectory rather than a hyperparameter.

\paragraph{Self-evolving agents, and what stops them.}
An agent can improve with no weight update, and this literature divides by what the
improvement is written into: natural language
\citep{selfrefine2023,reflexion2023,expel2024,echo2025hindsight,learningonthejob2026}, the
prompt \citep{promptbreeder2023,textgrad2024}, the scaffolding \citep{adas2024}, and code ---
the branch this paper belongs to, where evaluator-filtered variants gave way to autonomous
proposal and repair, self-rewriting coding agents, skill libraries, and whole research loops
\citep{funsearch2024,stop2023,generativeagents2023,voyager2023,alphaevolve2025,
selfimprovingcodingagent2025,darwingodelmachine2025,aiscientist2024,avo2026,
mendelgoedel2026}. Benchmarks alongside them measure such learning by transfer and
interference rather than retrieval, and separate the gain from the harness
\citep{agentcl2026,clbench2026,harnessengineering2026} --- hence ours is fixed. Ours carries
one artifact rather than a population, so experience is written into the program rather than
retrieved, which creates the failure mode measured here. The mechanism is accumulation:
revisions leave the program larger and more interconnected \citep{mccabe1976complexity}, and
each further edit must be placed without disturbing what works, until past some size
placement fails more often than it succeeds --- not for want of things to express, but
because the artifact has outgrown what its maintainer can hold, attribute, and change at
once. Every term of that is measured elsewhere. Erosion and verbosity rise across most
iterative trajectories without matching correctness, and human repositories degrade an order
of magnitude more slowly rather than being exempt, making capacity a rate and not an
agent-specific defect \citep{slopcodebench2026}; quality-aware prompting moves the intercept
and leaves the growth rate intact, pointing to a capacity rather than a prompting artifact.
Underneath, repair succeeds while accumulated context stays small
\citep{longcontextbugfix2026}, and per-step accuracy falls as steps accumulate even when the
plan is given, though reasoning removes the self-conditioning behind it
\citep{longhorizonexecution2026} --- so a plateau a reasoning agent reaches is not that
effect, and a program under revision cannot be decomposed away, the artifact \emph{being} the
accumulated context. One boundary stops these results from predicting our loop cannot work:
their degradation is driven by a growing specification, so nothing may be discarded, whereas
a game's rules are fixed and a better heuristic may \emph{replace} a worse one. Accumulation
is a tendency here, not an obligation, which is why the ceiling is worth measuring.
Improvement frequency and magnitude do decay on engineering tasks \citep{frontiereng2026},
but those are single-agent with neither a human field nor a learned baseline. EvoPolicyGym
is closer in protocol: it evaluates repeated executable-policy edits under a fixed episode
budget, hidden validation selection, held-out generalization, and trajectory-level feedback
diagnostics \citep{evopolicygym2026}. Its compact single-agent Gym suite and checkpoint-
selection objective still differ from our adversarial games, frozen opponent pool, and
one-artifact maintenance question; it therefore supplies a protocol precedent, not a
human or RL comparison. A survey
organizes the literature by the strength of the verification signal the loop closes on
\citep{selfimprovement2026survey}, at whose strong end a game engine with a frozen pool sits.
We carry that question into adversarial games, asking whether the plateau is explained by the
policy's maintainable description length rather than by the game's rule complexity.
